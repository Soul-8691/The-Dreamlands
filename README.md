# Lycantrope

**A 3D action-adventure fantasy RPG about a farmhand, a rusty sword, and every monster you've ever read about.**

Rowan Fenhallow carries grandfather's old sword because nobody else wanted it and it felt wrong to leave it behind. He mucks out the barn on Oswin Tull's farm, dreams strange dreams of a city in the clouds, and watches Aldmoor get a little worse each month — another cart that doesn't come back up the south road, another farm found empty. Then the sky tears open over Brackenvale's burying ground, and the sword wakes in his hand.

Lycantrope plays fantasy straight, with a wink. There's a prophecy, a wise mentor, a Dark Lord and four shards of a broken crown. There are rats in the cellar, goblins in the woods, a troll under the bridge, a wyrm in the deep. The title is the joke: it's not about werewolves (though there are some). It's a *Lycantrope*, a field guide to every fantasy trope with teeth.

The game will be **free on itch.io**, and its code is open source under the MIT licence.

> **Status: early development.** A first playable build is being made now. Most of what's below is the design, not finished game. See [what you can play today](#what-you-can-play-today).

## At a glance

- **Deliberate, fair combat.** Enemies show their wind-ups, attacks commit you, and every death teaches you something. Difficulty comes from timing, not from enemies with huge health bars.
- **An open world you navigate by sight.** If you can see it, you can go there. The Dark Lord's citadel stands under its storm cloud in the middle of the map, visible from everywhere.
- **The one who didn't run.** Aldmoor is a world already failing when the game opens — not a peaceful place that the raid destroys. Early areas feel dangerous because Rowan is a farmhand. Coming back to them later feels like a reckoning.
- **A monster for every trope.** You'll recognise every enemy at a glance, then learn something new about how it fights.
- **A two-sided story.** Half the journey is in the waking world; the other half is on the far side of sleep, in the Dreamlands.

## The story

### The waking world

A thousand years ago, a hero shattered the **Crown of Dawn** to seal **Malgrath the Undying** inside his citadel, Duskhold. The seal is failing. Malgrath's armies are spreading over **Aldmoor**, a fading kingdom of mossy ruins and wild country where the monsters outnumber the people. An old prophecy speaks of a hero "born of no house, carrying a forgotten blade."

Rowan was raised by **Oswin Tull**, a gruff, kind old farmer, after his grandfather died. Rowan has always dreamed of **Serannian**, a pink-marble city on the clouds. That's why the wandering wizard **Aldric Greymantle** comes looking for him. A mind that half-lives in dreams can stand at the mouth of a rift without breaking.

Aldric has a past he doesn't talk about. Thirty years ago he was a high priest of a drowned god on the Drowned Coast, until a warrior walked out of the sea-mist on the Night of the Rising Star, broke his rite, laid a strange blade to his throat and said: *"Not yet. You will be needed."* He has spent every year since learning to fight what he once served.

Rowan's training keeps getting cut short. Aldric speaks into his mind from afar, and Captain Mercy Thorne of the Watch teaches him steel and footwork. Each lesson is interrupted by the next attack:

1. **The burying ground.** The sky over Brackenvale's graves comes "unwritten." Small, furtive things pour out, and horned raiders step down out of the air. Oswin falls on his own doorstep defending the farm. He lives, and asks only whether anyone shut the gate.
2. **The stone circle.** A rift opens inside a ring of standing stones at night. Pale ghasts leap from the dark (they burn in light, so torches matter), and hooded cultists feed the rift. Behind it all stands **Aldric's old apprentice**, who has become the Dark Lord's champion, **Sir Calder the Black**.
3. **The galley from the clouds.** A black ship sails down out of the sky over a coastal town, trailing rifts like a wake. The last and largest rift, far out at sea, can't be closed from this side. Through it, Rowan sees Serannian, the city of his dreams, gone black.

Between the incursions, Rowan hunts the **Crown Shards** in the Wolfwood and the Blackmire, and the people of Brackenvale bring him their own troubles: a hill farm where the cattle vanish, a scholar's machine that shows things that shouldn't be there, a nightmare that has taken flesh in a cellar.

### The Dreamlands

Two of the Crown's shards fell through the Wall of Sleep when it shattered. So Rowan sleeps in the high house above the sea and walks down: seventy steps to the Cavern of Flame, seven hundred more to the Gate of Deeper Slumber. He carries the Dawnblade into dream.

The road runs through the Enchanted Wood, the cat-city of Ulthar, the harbour of Dylath-Leen, golden Celephaïs (where dreamers from other centuries share King Kuranes's table), the mountain Ngranek, the lightless Underworld of the ghouls, and the cold plateau of Leng. Rowan makes allies, or doesn't: the zoogs whose kin he frees, the cats of Ulthar whose favour he wins, the ghouls whose price he pays. Those who remember him come back for the end.

The two Dreamlands shards wait in the **ghouls' vault of fallen things** and in **Serannian** itself, where Rowan must seal the great rift from its far side over a night and a day, until the black drains out of the marble.

<details>
<summary><b>The Dawnblade's secret (spoilers)</b></summary>

The Dawnblade was never really an heirloom. Rowan's grandfather was a fisherman, and the morning after the Night of the Rising Star he hauled up a blade in his net that no whetstone would sharpen. Nobody would buy it, so it hung over the hearth.

A gift from Randolph Carter, the **Silver Key**, lets Rowan see through the eyes of its first wielder, the **hero of old**: the last knight of the order that once carried the Crown. In short echoes at old battlefields and shrines, the sword remembers techniques he taught it. At the end, the Key's last turn puts Rowan in the knight's body on the reef, on the Night of the Rising Star. He can't change what happens. He can only live it: spare the young priest, and give the blade to the tide with something of himself pressed into it. That's what finally wakes the sword for the fight with Malgrath.

And when Malgrath falls, a few small things don't add up. A laugh in another voice. A tall, dark stranger in the celebration crowd who isn't there when you look again. A last line in the mentor's testament, in a hand that isn't his. Someone was arranging all of it. The game never says who.

</details>

## The world

One seamless open world, with no loading screens between regions. Each region has a fixed level range; the world doesn't scale to you.

| Region | What it's like |
|---|---|
| **Brackenvale** (start) | Rolling meadows, farmsteads, a village with a well and a smithy, a ruined watchtower, standing stones. Travellers on the roads, cows in the fields. |
| **The Wolfwood** | Dense dark pine and fog, fallen giant trees, wolf dens and spider groves. |
| **The Blackmire** | A swamp of slowing water, stilt huts, sunken ruins and lantern-flies. |
| **Frostfang Peaks** | Snowy switchbacks, cliffs, ice caves and blizzards that shorten sight lines. |
| **The Ashen Reach** | Volcanic badlands, lava cracks, obsidian spires and an orc warcamp. |
| **The Sunken Crypts** | An underground network beneath the vale, full of the dead. |
| **Duskhold** | The Dark Lord's black fortress on a crag in the centre of the map. The last place you'll go. |
| **The Dreamlands** | The other side of sleep: Ulthar, Celephaïs, Leng, the Underworld, Serannian. |

**Exploring:**
- **Waystones** are where you rest. They heal you, refill your flask, save the game, and let you fast-travel between the ones you've lit. Resting also brings enemies back.
- **Shortcuts** (gates, ladders, levers) open from the far side, so the world keeps folding back to your last Waystone.
- **Secrets:** hidden walls that break with one hit, and chests only the Seer's Lens can reveal.
- **Dungeon tools:** each dungeon gives you a tool, such as a grappling chain, a lantern of revealing or iron boots, which opens new paths in the overworld.
- **Day, night and weather:** different monsters come out after dark (wraiths, werewolves), and each region has its own weather.
- **The world tells its own story:** item descriptions, ruined camps, letters and journals carry most of it. Some documents hold clues that open new dialogue or weaken a boss.

## Combat

Third-person, close and deliberate, in the tradition of the great 3D adventure games and the punishing action RPGs.

- **Lock on** to a target and circle it.
- **Light and heavy combos**, and **directional power attacks**: hold heavy and push forward for a lunge, back for a sweep, sideways for a cleave.
- **Stamina** for every swing, roll, block and sprint.
- **Dodge rolls** with a moment of invulnerability. How much you carry decides whether you roll fast, medium or slow, so heavy armour is a real trade-off.
- **Block** with a shield. Blocked hits drain stamina, and if it runs out your guard breaks.
- **Poise and stagger:** heavy hits break an enemy's poise and open them up. Yours too.
- **Plunge attacks** from ledges, **kicks** that break guards or knock enemies off edges, and **traps and falling rocks** that hurt them as well as you.
- **A hand crossbow** for knocking fliers out of the sky and interrupting spellcasters, with iron, blessed and fire bolts.
- **Throwables:** firebombs, holy water (hurts the undead) and smoke.
- **Rites,** a small set of spells cast from the off hand, such as a protective ward and a mark that makes a target take more damage. Each recharges on its own cooldown, so casting is part of the rhythm of every fight.
- **Lifewater,** a healing flask with a few charges, refilled at Waystones and upgradable.
- **Damage types and status effects:** slashing, blunt, piercing, fire, frost and radiant (holy light). Skeletons shrug off blades, trolls fear fire and wraiths fear the light. Bleed, poison, burn, frost and stun build up until they take hold.
- **Dying** loads your last save, and your experience goes back to what it was then. Experience is never dropped where you fell; it stays yours.
- **Hidden techniques,** found on scrolls in the world: a shield bash, a back-slice, a helm-splitter, a finishing stab.

**Mounted combat** on horseback is a stretch goal for later.

### Bosses

Every boss teaches or tests one idea, such as light against darkness, shooting down a diver, breaking a chant, or choosing not to fight. Each one has two phases, with a beat between them. If you die, you start again right outside the arena door, with no long walk back.

**Not every boss is a kill.** Some fights are won by surviving, sealing a rift, escaping, or sparing your opponent, and the game tells you so.

A few of the bosses:
- **The Thing in the Cellar:** a rat the size of a dog, fat on the farm's tallow. Your first boss.
- **Grimtooth, Alpha of the Wolfwood:** a giant werewolf who howls in his pack.
- **The Brood Queen:** a huge spider in a web cathedral, on the walls and the ceiling.
- **Old Mother Mire:** a bog hag riding a troll.
- **The Shantak Rider:** a sky-raider on a horse-headed bird, brought down from the air and finished on a cliff edge.
- **The Vault-Wyrm:** a blind wyrm in the ghouls' vault that hunts by sound.
- **The Black Marble Colossus:** Serannian's founder's statue, walking. Climb it, and each blow turns more of the marble white again.
- **Malgrath the Undying:** sorcerer-king, then something much larger.

## Growing stronger

- **Experience from kills,** scaled by how dangerous the enemy is for your level, and from finding landmarks and Waystones. Level up anywhere, any time you're out of combat: each level is one attribute point, up to level 50.
- **Death costs nothing you'd saved:** experience is never dropped, but dying loads your last save, so any XP earned since then goes with it.
- **Six attributes:**
  - **Might:** heavy weapons, breaking poise.
  - **Dexterity:** light, precise weapons, the crossbow, faster recovery.
  - **Vigor:** health.
  - **Endurance:** stamina and how much you can carry.
  - **Faith:** radiant damage, Rites and their cooldowns, healing.
  - **Fortune:** critical hits and loot.
- **Skills improve by use:** Blade, Blunt, Polearm, Marksman, Guard, Evasion, Athletics and Rites. Land hits to get better at hitting; dodge well to get better at dodging.
- **Perks** at every 10 skill levels. Choose one of two, such as longer invulnerability on rolls or rolls that cost less stamina.
- **Attunement slots** decide how many Rites and relic powers you can carry into a fight.

### The Dawnblade

The sword starts rusty and dull. Each Crown Shard restores part of it: a new glow, a new edge, and a new technique (a dash-thrust, a rising slash, a charged plunge, a crescent sweep). You can see your progress on the weapon itself.

The shards also grant **relic powers** on a cooldown, such as a gale that blasts enemies back, a frost that freezes attackers, a ground slam and a ring of fire. You'll also find the **Seer's Lens**, which reveals hidden things for a few seconds, and **trophy masks** taken from fallen bosses, each lending you one of its owner's traits.

### Gear

- **Weapons:**
  - **Classes:** swords, axes, maces, spears and greatswords, each with its own moveset and scaling.
  - **One-handed and two-handed:** a main hand, an off hand and a sidearm, and any weapon can be held two-handed.
  - **Magic implements:** staffs, talismans, censers and tomes.
  - **Upgrades and sigil slots:** restore them at the smith with material found in the world, and socket carved sigils for elemental effects.
- **Armour** comes in sets, from farmhand's patched coat to chainmail, wolfhide, ranger's leathers, watch plate, orcish bonemail and the Dark Lord's own dread plate. Pieces show on Rowan as you wear them, and full sets give bonuses.

## The bestiary

The in-game bestiary is a book called ***The Lycantrope: A Field Guide to Monsters Most Common***, written by a snarky travelling scholar. Its entries poke fun at each monster's trope and still give you real tactics.

Expect the classics, levelled from the meadows to the mountains:
- **Lowlands:** giant rats, grey wolves, goblin cutthroats and hexers, bandits, wild boars, bog oozes.
- **Deeper in:** skeleton warriors and archers, giant spiders, lizardfolk, orc berserkers, gnoll raiders, crypt ghouls, will-o'-wisps, cockatrices.
- **The dangerous ones:** bog trolls, wraiths, frost yetis, werewolves, ogres, dread knights, mimic chests, necromancers, banshees, basilisks.
- **The end of the scale:** hill giants, a swamp hydra, an ash drake and a lich.

Behind the rifts and across the Wall come stranger things, drawn from H. P. Lovecraft's public-domain Dreamlands stories:
- **Raiders and their servants:** furtive zoogs; horned, hoofed Men of Leng; pale toad-like moon-beasts who command them.
- **In the dark:** ghasts that die in the light; dog-faced ghouls who might become friends; gugs too huge to follow you into a tunnel.
- **Things you shouldn't fight:** silent night-gaunts, and a colour out of space that drains the land around it grey.

Some of them only matter if you make the right choices: free the zoogs instead of killing them, and they'll fight beside you.

## Sound and music

- **Voices:** every character, from Rowan to the goblins, has a voice of their own, and enemies call out as they fight.
- **Subtitles** for every voice line.
- **Music:**
  - **Exploration:** a slow, sweeping orchestral score in the spirit of classic open-world fantasy.
  - **Combat:** music that rises when something is hunting you.
  - **Places:** each region gets its own track, every boss its own theme, and there's a heroic main theme.

## How it's made

Lycantrope is made by one developer with the help of AI tools, and says so openly:
- **3D models** are generated with **Meshy** and **Tripo3D**.
- **Voices, sound effects and music** are generated with **ElevenLabs**.
- **The landscape, textures, sky and dungeon pieces** are generated by Python scripts.

Hand work fixes things rather than creating them. Every generated asset is credited, and the itch.io page includes an AI disclosure.

**Inspirations:**
- Ocarina of Time, Majora's Mask and Twilight Princess;
- Morrowind, Oblivion and Skyrim;
- Dark Souls 1–3;
- The Witcher 1–3;
- the public-domain stories of H. P. Lovecraft, especially *The Dream-Quest of Unknown Kadath*.

All names and characters in the game are original, except the public-domain ones from Lovecraft's stories.

## What you can play today

The current build has the world, the full combat foundation and the core progression loop:

- **Explore:** Brackenvale and the country beyond. Farmsteads, lanes, villagers and travellers on the roads, cows in the fields, solid houses, fences, walls, trees and grass.
- **Fight:** wolves, bears, goblins, bandits and battlemages. Light and heavy attacks, blocking, strafing while locked on, and a Rite. Catching an enemy mid-wind-up hits 25% harder.
- **Feel the numbers:** every hit is calculated from the full stat model. Stamina costs, poise breaks, per-type armour defence, elemental absorb and status buildup are all live.
- **Grow:** earn XP from kills and discoveries; spend it on the level-up screen. Heal with the Lifewater flask or an apple.
- **Mind the drop:** falls under 4 m are free; beyond that, damage ramps to lethal. Roll out of the landing (press C just before you hit), splash into deep water, or plunge onto an enemy below and take nothing.
- **Listen:** the town music changes as day becomes night. Combat music rises when something is hunting you.

## Coming next

Roughly in priority order. All of these have approved design entries in the idea tracker.

**Core loops that are missing:**
- Save files and main menu — three save slots, saves at Waystones and on quit.
- Pause menu, inventory and equipment — open inventory, swap armour pieces and weapons, quick-slot items.
- Item pickup — press [E] near a chest or dropped item; a brief pop-in shows what you got.
- NPC talk trigger — press [E] to speak; first-approach lines for key characters trigger automatically.
- Quest log — a one-page list in the pause menu, populated by NPC rumours. Pin one entry at a time to show a compass marker on the HUD, Skyrim-style.

**Brackenvale:**
- Jory's merchant stall — buy consumables, sell found gear. Closes when Jory goes home.
- NPC daily schedules — named villagers go home after dark and return to their posts in the morning.
- Guard response AI — Watch guards engage enemies that cross the village fence.
- Beginning plot / cold open — the morning before the first raid (tutorial movement and interaction, then the sky tears).

**World and feel:**
- Enemy camps as overworld POIs — goblin and bandit camps with a captain, a chest and a reason to exist.
- Brackenvale surroundings layout pass — deliberate placement of the first goblin camp, wolf den, burying ground and Brack Tor within 400 m of the village so the opening reads as a world, not a test bench.
- More tree variety and denser forest canopy — enemies in the Wolfwood shouldn't be visible from open meadow.
- Terrain relief pass — more pronounced hills and ridges outside Brackenvale.
- Biome colour palette pass — each region gets a distinct warm/cool cast so the sky, ground and fog all agree.

## Design direction

A periodic review of the approved ideas against comparable games flagged two risks. The full analysis is in [RPG_CLONES.md](RPG_CLONES.md). Short version:

**Fable** (2003): the original story structure — peaceful village, morning chore, raid, sword grabbed from the hearth, wise mentor who falls mid-game, "from nobody to legend" arc — is Fable 1's plot beat for beat. The fix is making Aldmoor feel *already in decline* before Rowan is involved, which costs nothing in code and is already reflected in the revised opening paragraph and "at a glance" framing above. A dialogue pass over the Brackenvale NPC lines (making their worry show *under* their small talk rather than through comedic whimsy) is the other half.

**RuneScape**: the weapon modification system was labelled "rune sockets" — renamed to "sigil slots" above. Use-based skill growth should stay invisible while happening; only perk milestones surface. Weapon upgrades are framed as *restoring* a sword, not crafting a new tier, and the upgrade material will have a name specific to Aldmoor rather than generic "ore."

None of these changes affect the systems already built. They're wording, tone and naming corrections.
